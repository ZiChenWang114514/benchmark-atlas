# GPBench

A genomic prediction benchmark covering 16 species, 57 traits and 40,821 samples, evaluating 21 methods under a unified protocol for effectiveness, sensitivity, robustness and scalability.

## Design goals

**Capabilities tested**：Compares genomic prediction methods under a unified protocol along effectiveness, sensitivity, robustness and scalability, and adds a large language model system for interpreting experiments and recommending methods

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

**Gap in prior benchmarks**：The paper argues that the field still faces dataset bias and limited representativeness, the lack of standardized evaluation protocols and a systematic benchmarking framework, and heavy reliance on expert experience to interpret results, which hinders systematic cross-species comparison

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

## Construction

**Question sources**：Public genotype-phenotype datasets were collected and harmonized across animals, plants and microorganisms, then passed through quality control, preprocessing and genotype encoding to form a unified benchmark. The full species data are also deposited at figshare record 10.6084/m9.figshare.31007608

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

**Author background**：Bioinformatics and data-mining researchers from Shandong University, Beijing University of Civil Engineering and Architecture, New York University Shanghai, George Mason University and China Agricultural University

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

**Review process**：The preprint has not been certified by peer review; the bioRxiv page records that the authors declared no competing interests

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

**Quality control**：Appendix A.1 describes per-species genotype quality control, including removal of sites with low minor-allele frequency or high missingness and, for some datasets, linkage-disequilibrium pruning. Before splitting, each dataset is shuffled with fixed random seed 42, and all methods share the same partitions; methods with hyperparameters use Optuna in a nested cross-validation scheme

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

**Decontamination**：Not applicable (the task compares genomic prediction models on published public datasets, and is not a training-contamination audit of language questions)

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

## Data format

**Size**：Both the paper abstract and the official platform state 16 species, 57 traits and 40,821 samples. Appendix A.1 lists the 16 species as cattle, chicken, chickpea, cotton, horse, human, loblolly pine, maize, millet, mouse, pig, rapeseed, rice, soybean, wheat and yeast. The paper abstract reports 493,821 single-nucleotide polymorphisms, matching the sum of retained markers across the Appendix A.1 datasets; the official platform page states 49,771,695 single-nucleotide polymorphisms, and the two figures do not agree

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

**Question types**：Genomic prediction tasks: most are regression on continuous traits; human breast cancer and age-related macular degeneration are discrete disease classification

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

**Modalities**：Genome-wide single-nucleotide polymorphism genotypes and phenotypic records

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

**Languages**：Not applicable (the data are genotypes and phenotypes, not natural-language questions); the companion analysis tool supports queries in Chinese and English

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

**Difficulty**：Predictive performance varies substantially across species and traits. The paper reports that statistical genetics methods are stable and competitive on most species, that feature-engineering tree models rank near the top on several evaluation dimensions, and that sequence-based deep learning models show substantial variability, with limited robustness and generalization

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

**Public and hidden split**：All datasets use 10-fold cross-validation; each dataset is randomly shuffled with fixed seed 42 before splitting, and all compared methods share the same partitions. Methods with hyperparameters use nested cross-validation: Optuna selects hyperparameters in the inner loop by Pearson correlation or F1, and the outer 10-fold loop provides unbiased estimates

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

## Evaluation

**Metrics**：Regression tasks use Pearson correlation, mean absolute error, the coefficient of determination, root mean squared error and the consistency index; classification tasks use accuracy, macro-averaged precision, recall and F1. Evaluation is also organized along effectiveness, sensitivity, robustness and scalability, with runtime and peak CPU or GPU memory recorded

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

**Scoring**：Each method is trained and tested on the same 10-fold splits, writing per-fold ground-truth and predicted values and reporting 10-fold average metrics. Sensitivity analyses further vary sample size, feature dimension and random seeds (42, 123 and 246)

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

**Prompt settings**：Not applicable (genomic prediction model evaluation). The companion large language model analysis tool requires a user-configured model interface and is used for result interpretation and method recommendation; it is not part of the quantitative comparison of the 21 methods

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

**Official scripts**：https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

**Comparability note**：The paper abstract's marker total of 493,821 matches the sum of retained sites in Appendix A.1; the official platform page states 49,771,695, a different figure whose accounting could not be reconciled from the fetched sources, so the two numbers should not be mixed. Table 1 summarizes Pearson correlation and the consistency index by species, and the species listed in that table header are not identical to the 16 species in the appendix. This benchmark evaluates genomic prediction algorithms and is not the same task as existing genome-language benchmarks such as Genome-Bench

Sources: [1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.31.762999v1) [2](https://www.sdu-idea.cn/GPBench/) [3](https://github.com/xwzhang2118/GPBench) (accessed 2026-11-11)

**Results snapshot**：The paper reports that Table 1 shows statistical genetics methods to be stable and competitive on most species, that feature-engineering methods, especially tree models, rank near the top on several dimensions and species, and that sequence-based deep learning models vary substantially, with limited robustness and generalization. In Table 1, rrBLUP attains a Pearson correlation of 0.781 and a consistency index of 0.482 on the cattle dataset. **Coverage note**：The framework evaluates 21 methods: five statistical genetics methods (rrBLUP, GBLUP, BayesA, BayesB, BayesC), five feature-engineering machine learning methods (ElasticNet, SVR, random forest, XGBoost, LightGBM) and 11 sequence-representation deep learning methods. The official platform is a client-rendered single-page application, so detailed ranking numbers require a browser load; this filing follows the paper and the code repository
