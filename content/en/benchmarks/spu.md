# SPU

Science Prediction Universe comprises 100 public tasks from 85 Nature Portfolio papers and 16 private unpublished wet-lab tasks; the paper reports task-adjusted Spearman means from 0.227 to 0.478 across 13 agent configurations on the public set.

## Design goals

**Capabilities tested**：Tests whether an agent, given the setup of a real wet-lab experiment together with candidate conditions and the measurement protocol, can rank hidden measured outcomes. Tasks span chemistry, materials science, biology, and medicine, with endpoints such as reaction yields, ionic conductivities, mechanical strength, fluorescence signals, and RNA-seq gene counts.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Gap in prior benchmarks**：The paper argues that existing outcome-prediction benchmarks mostly use single question-answer calls without tools and therefore measure what a model already knows, while agentic science or container-workflow benchmarks target analysis of data already in hand or deterministic tests, rather than prediction of wet-lab measurements withheld from the predictor.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

## Construction

**Question sources**：The 100 public tasks are built from 85 papers published online between January 2025 and August 2026 in 11 Nature Portfolio journals; 70 papers contribute one task each and 15 contribute two. Most appeared in Nature Communications (41 papers, 43 tasks) or Nature (28 papers, 40 tasks). The 16 private tasks come from four series of unpublished wet-lab experiments carried out in collaboration with scientific laboratories.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Author background**：The leading institution is UniPat AI. Advisory-committee members are affiliated with Stanford University, Cornell University, FutureHouse, The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, Peking University, Harvard University, The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou), University of Illinois Chicago, and Shanghai University, among others. Wet-lab collaborators are from Yurogen Biosystems LLC, ABclonal Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Deep Principle, and Bota Biosciences. More than ten experts in biology, chemistry, materials science, medicine, and computer science took part in building the public tasks.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Review process**：Before being frozen, each public task was reviewed against the original sources independently of its construction, and any blocking issue was fixed and reviewed again. Each private task also passed an independent review before it was frozen, and no model scores were used to decide which experiments the private tasks include.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Quality control**：Domain experts curated source studies exclusively from recent Nature Portfolio journals that report complete quantitative measurements, and avoided comparisons that rise or fall strictly with one varied parameter or whose values differ only slightly within a range more likely to reflect experimental error. After pilot runs, 100 public tasks were selected as one task in each of 29 subfields plus 71 more chosen for scientific quality, nontrivial computational demand, and informative difficulty. Measurements in the four private series used independent experimental replicates or same-plate replicate wells. Grading is automatic and deterministic in an isolated container; no model judges a submission.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Decontamination**：Public tasks use four safeguards: an isolated grader with anonymized tasks; an information policy with audited tools and a stop-on-exposure rule; automated screening of tool outputs for source identifiers; and private tasks whose results cannot be found online. The agent is forbidden from using the source study, its supplementary materials and data, and any result of the target or same-type experiments, whether found online or reconstructed from memory, as well as predictors fitted to such results. The paper reports that in no configuration did runs flagged for encountering source-study identifiers score significantly higher than unflagged runs after multiple-testing correction; the 16 unpublished private tasks provide a check that does not depend on the detector.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

## Data format

**Size**：116 tasks in total: 100 public and 16 private. The benchmark has 4,207 experiments in 517 groups, of which the public tasks account for 3,947 experiments and 489 groups and the private tasks 260 experiments in 28 groups. Public tasks comprise 39 chemistry, 29 materials science, 20 biology, and 12 medicine tasks across 29 subfields; all 116 tasks comprise 42 chemistry, 31 materials science, 31 biology, and 12 medicine tasks in 32 subfields. A task contains 4 to 362 experiments (median 16) in 1 to 117 comparison groups.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Question types**：The agent must submit a strict ranking from lowest to highest measured value for each comparison group and does not have to predict the values themselves. The submission file is rankings.json, together with a report, a source record, and supporting scripts. Of the 100 public tasks, 97 weight their groups equally; 33 public tasks contain tied measured values.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Modalities**：Text plus symbolic representations such as structures or sequences; some tasks include structure images. The Hugging Face dataset card labels the modalities as text and image.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Languages**：English (according to the language tag on the Hugging Face dataset card)

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Difficulty**：Task size varies widely. Across public tasks the chance threshold has a median of 0.44, and it lies between 0.71 and 0.80 for the 17 tasks with fewer than seven experiments. Final public-task selection used scientific quality, nontrivial computational demand, and informative difficulty, with difficulty judged mainly from pilot scores of GPT-6 Astra.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Public and hidden split**：The 100 public tasks are released on Hugging Face. The dataset card states that reference answers, measurements, graders, and oracle solutions are included, so these tasks are not a held-out test set. The 16 private tasks are not publicly released and are withheld for controlled evaluation.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

## Evaluation

**Metrics**：The primary metric is the Spearman rank correlation between the submitted ranking and the measured values in each comparison group, combined with fixed group weights that sum to one to yield the task score, which lies in [-1, 1]; a uniformly random valid ranking has expected score 0. The paper also reports a raw mean, a task-adjusted mean, the fraction of tasks above the chance threshold, and coverage. The chance threshold is the 95th percentile of 4,000 random valid submissions.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Scoring**：Grading is automatic and deterministic in a separate container without network access and does not score the agent's report. A missing or wholly invalid file scores -1; an error confined to one group sets that group's correlation to -1 while keeping its weight. Because 33 public tasks contain tied measurements, their attainable maximum is slightly below 1 (0.956 at the lowest). To separate scientific accuracy from safety refusals and formatting errors, the main analysis scores a run when its rankings can be unambiguously recovered from a formatting-rejected file; safety refusals and runs without a recoverable submission are excluded from the mean and reported via task coverage.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Prompt settings**：All configurations receive the same task instructions and run in a container with 8 CPU cores, 16 GiB of memory, one NVIDIA H20 GPU, and an eight-hour budget, and may search the web, install software, and compute freely. Experiments carry only anonymized identifiers, and the agent is not told the source study. GPT-6 Astra runs in the Codex CLI (v0.156.1) at medium and max reasoning effort, GPT-6.1 Sol runs in the same Codex version at max effort, and the other ten models run in Claude Code (v2.1.260) at max reasoning effort.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Official scripts**：The public tasks are released as Harbor tasks. The official dataset card and code repository give example Harbor commands to run a task in Docker, and state that in the paper each run had 8 CPU cores, 16 GiB of memory, one NVIDIA H20 GPU, web access, and an eight-hour budget, with the grader running in a separate container without network access.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Comparability note**：The scores below are official, paper-reported evaluations, not third-party reproductions. The task-adjusted mean is fitted to the scores of all thirteen evaluated configurations and cannot be computed from a single run. The dataset card states that the paper's audited web tools and source-identifier screening are not part of this release, so results obtained without them are not directly comparable to the paper's. When reporting results, give the mean over scored tasks, the number of scored tasks, the model version, reasoning effort, and harness.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15049) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15049.pdf) [3](https://spu.unipat.ai/) [4](https://github.com/UniPat-AI/SPU) [5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UnipatAI/SPU) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Coverage note**：Of 1,300 official public runs, 1,155 are scored; 130 of 145 exclusions are safety refusals, nearly all from Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 (scored on 44 and 30 tasks, mostly in materials science). Of 208 private runs, 159 are scored. Figure 1 also reports unadjusted means over scored public and private tasks, which should not be mixed with the task-adjusted means in Table 1. Raw means by discipline are in Table 5; the paper describes those comparisons as descriptive.
