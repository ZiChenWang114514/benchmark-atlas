# LiveGeo

LiveGeo comprises 1,237 task instances (1,088 static and 149 matched dynamic); the paper reports that the best overall success rate among nine models is 27.8% for Gemini 3.7 Flash, with mean success on matched dynamic settings falling 70.3% and 79.3% relative to the static baseline.

## Design goals

**Capabilities tested**：Tests whether general-purpose LLM agents can make reliable decisions on real-world urban geography: acquire task-relevant information, check whether evidence supporting earlier decisions remains valid, and revise plans when the world state changes. Tasks cover place grounding, place discovery, and mobility planning, as well as multi-entity, long-horizon, safety-aware, and region-aware compositional decisions, evaluated in matched static and dynamic settings.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Gap in prior benchmarks**：The paper argues that existing evaluations largely reason and plan over fixed geographic snapshots; even benchmarks that introduce dynamics often focus on specific urban processes or predefined disruptions that are explicitly exposed to the agent, and therefore do not test whether agents can keep decisions grounded in still-valid evidence while the external world evolves on its own temporal processes.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

## Construction

**Question sources**：Tasks are built on real-world urban geographic instantiations in the GeoWorld platform. The evaluation covers six cities—Shanghai, Tokyo, Singapore, London, New York, and Cape Town—with 88 regions, 1,376 points of interest, and 107,372 cached routes. Static tasks start from a feasible reference solution and constraints derived from geographic facts, which an LLM then writes as a natural-language request; dynamic tasks keep the same initial world state, constraints, reference solution, and user request, then inject validated temporal events that change the reference solution, yielding matched pairs.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Author background**：Author affiliations are the National University of Singapore, Zhejiang University, Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications, and the University of Oxford. The paper does not provide further personal background on the authors.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Review process**：Natural-language requests and dynamic events are reviewed by human annotators before inclusion. The appendix states that all candidate instances are independently inspected by three human reviewers, who check instantiated entities and world state, task constraints, the reference solution, supporting evidence, and, for dynamic tasks, event-timeline consistency; inclusion requires unanimous approval. Table 5 reports review pass rates of 92.5% for static candidates overall and 89.2% for dynamic candidates overall.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Quality control**：Static tasks use programmatic checks and constraint solvers to verify that the reference solution satisfies all constraints, that the constraints are grounded in the world state, and that the information needed to solve the task is accessible through the agent's tools. Dynamic tasks verify temporal consistency of the event sequence, validity of the reference solutions under the corresponding world states, and observability of task-relevant changes through the tools. The pipeline also includes automated checks (facts and feasibility, leakage and duplicates) and human review of maps, requests, and tool-instruction clarity. Evaluation uses predefined output contracts and task-specific constraint checks, with state-aligned interaction traces retained.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Decontamination**：The construction quality-control steps include leakage and duplicate checks; reference solutions are withheld from the agent. The paper does not describe a dedicated decontamination procedure against pretraining corpora.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

## Data format

**Size**：1,237 verifiable task instances in seven categories: 1,088 static instances (478 atomic: 132 Grounding, 171 Discovery, 175 Mobility Planning; 610 compositional: 176 Multi-Entity, 150 Long-Horizon, 121 Safety-Aware, 163 Region-Aware) and 149 matched dynamic instances (23 Mobility Planning, 32 Multi-Entity, 25 Long-Horizon, 34 Safety-Aware, 35 Region-Aware). The GeoWorld evaluation instantiation covers 6 cities, 88 regions, 1,376 points of interest, and 107,372 cached routes, and provides agents with 8 public App namespaces and 31 tools.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Question types**：Agents query the environment, advance virtual time, and submit decisions in task-specific formats through the unified GeoWorld interface. Output contracts vary by task: for example, Grounding requires an explicit location candidate before the final answer, Mobility Planning requires feasibility, route identifiers, and travel mode, and Long-Horizon requires a day-wise itinerary. Dynamic tasks unfold within a bounded virtual-time window on a predefined event sequence; agents decide when to query, advance time, and revisit earlier decisions.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Modalities**：Primarily text; Grounding tasks use visual or textual clues, including street-view clues, to identify a place, so the evaluation includes both visual and textual inputs.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Languages**：English (based on the paper's example user requests and evaluation setup)

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Difficulty**：The paper states that current agents remain far from solving geographic decision-making even on atomic tasks. Average success rates of the nine models on the seven static categories are 23.5% for Grounding, 24.1% for Discovery, 22.0% for Mobility Planning, 30.8% for Multi-Entity, 3.4% for Long-Horizon, and 12.9% for Safety-Aware; even the best model reaches only 8.7% on Long-Horizon. On 149 matched tasks, mean success drops from 19.1% under Static to 5.7% under Task-Only Dynamics and 4.0% under Shared Dynamics.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Public and hidden split**：The paper distinguishes public task materials (request, media, and App state) from a private reference (answer, proof, and provenance). Official documentation points the task data to the LiveGeo-benchmark repository; as of a 2026-11-14 query, that repository contained only a placeholder README, so whether the full public data have been uploaded is unverified.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

## Evaluation

**Metrics**：Task success is determined by task-specific verification criteria covering four main aspects, with the applicable aspects varying by category: Decision Correctness (whether the final decision meets correctness criteria, including place selection and feasibility judgments), Plan Executability (whether the decisions satisfy spatial, temporal, travel-mode, and other operational constraints), Evidence Support (whether the interaction history contains observations that substantiate the decisions), and, for dynamic tasks, Dynamic Adaptability (whether agents revalidate and, when necessary, revise decisions in response to task-relevant world changes). Table 3 reports success rates in percent with standard errors of the mean. The introduction also states the strongest atomic-task result as pass@1.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Scoring**：Each task instance is evaluated over three independent runs. Dynamic evaluations use the same replayed world timeline and event schedule for all models. In Table 3, bold and underline mark the best and second-best distinct scores in each column (ties share a mark). The main dynamic settings use pull-based information access; the appendix also introduces an Explicit Update setting in which public event notifications are appended directly to the system context.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Prompt settings**：Nine models are evaluated under a unified ReAct-based agent scaffold and tool-access protocol, with a maximum of 200 interaction steps per task, temperature 0.1, a maximum generation length of 10K tokens per turn, and full interaction context retained (all evaluated models support context windows of at least 128K tokens). Task-specific output contracts are identical across models on the same task. Table 18 lists the API model identifiers: gpt-5.6, claude-opus-5, claude-sonnet-5, gemini-3.1-pro-preview, gemini-3.7-flash, grok-4.5, qwen3.8-max, kimi-k3, and deepseek-v4-flash-vision-exp.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Official scripts**：The GeoWorld repository provides a local demo, an evaluation guide, and test commands, including launching World Studio after a Python install and running pytest. The paper states that detailed model versions, prompts, output contracts, and runtime configurations are in Appendix C.1.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)

**Comparability note**：The scores below are author-reported in the paper, obtained under a unified scaffold, three independent runs, and temperature 0.1, among other settings. The official task repository contained only a placeholder README at the time of this check, so third parties without the full task data cannot be compared directly with the paper's results.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17267) [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17267.pdf) [3](https://livegeoworld.github.io/) [4](https://github.com/LiveGeoWorld/GeoWorld) (accessed 2026-11-14)
