# SciBench

> Verification date: 2026-11-08. The access date is not the publication date or the run date; a field that cannot be confirmed remains Unverified.

SciBench measures multi-step reasoning, knowledge of the domain, and numerical work in scientific computing from university textbooks and in course examinations. Understanding it requires keeping apart the textbook counts in the paper, the text now released on HF, the subset that contains figures, and the closed-book examinations. Scoring is also not a requirement that the strings be identical. A numeric answer taken from a textbook is allowed a relative tolerance.

Guide source: [Official materials](https://scibench-ucla.github.io/) (accessed 2026-11-08); specific facts and historical access dates are given in the individual fields.

## Construction

### 题目或数据来源

University exercises in mathematics, physics, and chemistry are collected from instructional textbooks; an error-attribution protocol is also designed, combining human annotation with model-assisted analysis.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10635) (accessed 2026-11-08); [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.10635) (accessed 2026-11-08); [3](https://github.com/mandyyyyii/scibench) (accessed 2026-11-07)

### 出题背景

The research team collected questions from textbooks commonly used at universities and from course exams authorized by the instructors; human annotators took part in OCR transcription, checking, and error attribution.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.10635) (accessed 2026-11-08)

### Review process

Textbook PDFs are converted by Mathpix OCR into LaTeX, and compilation, the numeric value and LaTeX of the answer, and units that stand alone are checked by hand; a question with several parts keeps the first part or is split into independent questions. Course examinations were authorized by the teachers who taught the course.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.10635) (accessed 2026-11-08)

### Quality control

Errors of the model and of the standard solution are analyzed from human annotation, and an LLM verifier then attributes them to ten abilities in scientific problem solving; this is a protocol for error analysis, and it is not proof that the question bank is free of error.

Sources: [1](https://github.com/mandyyyyii/scibench) (accessed 2026-11-07)

### 去污染措施

Official selection of questions requires that an item not be easy to obtain or extract as text online, so as to reduce the risk of leakage; this does not mean that an audit of model training data has been completed.

Sources: [1](https://scibench-ucla.github.io/) (accessed 2026-11-08)

## Evaluation

### Metrics

Textbook questions are evaluated by the accuracy of the numerical answer, with a relative error of 5% allowed; course examinations are scored against a teacher rubric; attribution of errors to ten abilities is a separate analysis, not another accuracy.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.10635) (accessed 2026-11-08)

### Scoring

Numerical answers are compared with the reference value, at a relative tolerance of 5%; the exponent of scientific notation and the physical unit are stored separately, and exact matching of strings cannot be treated as the official protocol. Examination solutions follow a teacher rubric.

Sources: [1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.10635) (accessed 2026-11-08)

### 提示与运行设置

The repository lists settings including zero_nosys, zero, zeroCot, few, fewCot, python, and wolfram; tool setups and prompting setups must be compared separately.

Sources: [1](https://github.com/mandyyyyii/scibench) (accessed 2026-11-07)
