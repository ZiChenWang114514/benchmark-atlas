## Design goals

**Capabilities tested**：Tests whether scientific agents can make sequential decisions from experimental feedback under budgets and constraints, covering experimental optimization, safety-constraint adherence, adaptive robustness, and multimodal spectral reasoning from spectra and images.

Sources: [1](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam/blob/main/paper/AI_Scientists_Last_Exam.pdf) [2](https://aisle.science/) (accessed 2026-11-10)

**Gap in prior benchmarks**：The paper argues that scientific question-answering benchmarks and agent benchmarks in software environments do not systematically measure repeated decisions, feedback interpretation, cost and noise, or timely adaptation under irreversible physical conditions.

Sources: [1](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam/blob/main/paper/AI_Scientists_Last_Exam.pdf) (accessed 2026-11-10)

## Construction

**Question sources**：The first round uses low-cost physical proxy experiments with dyes and indicators, plus matched simulated tasks. Tasks include colour-matching optimization, safety-constrained colour matching, recovery from hidden pipetting drift, resin transfer, active pKa inference, and spectral matching.

Sources: [1](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam/blob/main/paper/AI_Scientists_Last_Exam.pdf) [2](https://aisle.science/) (accessed 2026-11-10)

**Author background**：Written collaboratively by laboratories in automated experimentation, computational chemistry, and scientific intelligence. Co-lead institutes are UNSW, NUS, EPFL, and UC Berkeley.

Sources: [1](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam/blob/main/paper/AI_Scientists_Last_Exam.pdf) [2](https://mittrchina.com/news/detail/17220) (accessed 2026-11-10)

## Data format

**Size**：The first round reports 4,079 real wet-lab experiments and 46,626 minutes of instrument runtime across two automated sites. Dry and wet leaderboards are separate. Tasks include 4- and 8-component colour matching, colour matching with forbidden regions, hidden drift, resin transfer, active pKa inference, and spectral matching.

Sources: [1](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam/blob/main/paper/AI_Scientists_Last_Exam.pdf) [2](https://aisle.science/) [3](https://feng.ifeng.com/c/8xupEkUBbFb) (accessed 2026-11-10)

**Question types**：Closed-loop interaction: the model proposes an action, equipment or a simulator executes it and returns observations, and the model continues within budget or submits a final answer.

Sources: [1](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam/blob/main/paper/AI_Scientists_Last_Exam.pdf) [2](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam) (accessed 2026-11-10)

**Modalities**：Text instructions, formulation and volume actions, colour and spectral measurements, spectrum images, and observation feedback in simulation.

Sources: [1](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam/blob/main/paper/AI_Scientists_Last_Exam.pdf) [2](https://aisle.science/) (accessed 2026-11-10)

**Languages**：Repository documentation and task instructions are primarily in English.

Sources: [1](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam) (accessed 2026-11-10)

**Difficulty**：The paper and homepage show low overall scores for current models on real wet-lab tasks; hard colour-matching targets are rarely met, and the complete safety pass rate is 0%.

Sources: [1](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam/blob/main/paper/AI_Scientists_Last_Exam.pdf) [2](https://aisle.science/) (accessed 2026-11-10)

**Public and hidden split**：Task environments and verifiers are public on GitHub and Hugging Face. Real wet-lab runs need automated-lab access; simulated tasks can be reproduced locally.

Sources: [1](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam) [2](https://huggingface.co/datasets/AISLE-science/ai-scientists-last-exam) [3](https://aisle.science/) (accessed 2026-11-10)

## Evaluation

**Metrics**：Separate scores for optimization, safety, adaptive robustness, and multimodal spectral reasoning, aggregated into wet-lab and dry-lab totals on a 0–100 scale.

Sources: [1](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam/blob/main/paper/AI_Scientists_Last_Exam.pdf) [2](https://aisle.science/) (accessed 2026-11-10)

**Scoring**：Task-specific metrics are aggregated hierarchically. Models with complete wet-lab coverage and complete dry-lab coverage are ranked separately. The safety dimension records forbidden-region violations together with task progress.

Sources: [1](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam/blob/main/paper/AI_Scientists_Last_Exam.pdf) (accessed 2026-11-10)

**Prompt settings**：The first wet-lab round evaluates multiple frontier large language models. The public board lists wet and dry sub-scores. Prompt templates follow the task packages in the repository.

Sources: [1](https://aisle.science/) [2](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam) [3](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam/blob/main/paper/AI_Scientists_Last_Exam.pdf) (accessed 2026-11-10)

**Official scripts**：Task runners and verifiers built on the Harbor framework; the repository provides harbor run examples and wetlab framework notes.

Sources: [1](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam) (accessed 2026-11-10)

**Comparability note**：Scores come from the project’s first evaluation and public board, not from an independent third-party retest. Wet and dry results, and models with incomplete coverage, should not be mixed casually.

Sources: [1](https://aisle.science/) [2](https://github.com/MasterAI-EAM/ai-scientists-last-exam/blob/main/paper/AI_Scientists_Last_Exam.pdf) (accessed 2026-11-10)
